\newcommand{\fullpsalm}[1]{%
	% \index[P]{\csname psalm#1Incipit\endcsname}
	\begingroup % Start local group to limit scope
		\clubpenalty=10000 % SET HIGH to protect the Lettrine
		\setlength{\parindent}{.25em}
		\let\psalmtext\empty
		\expandafter\let\expandafter\psalmtext\csname psalm#1Text\endcsname 
		\newif\ifblue
		\bluetrue
		\firstversetrue % Reset toggle for the new psalm
		\newcommand{\processline}[1]{%
			\StrLeft{##1}{1}[\firstletter]%
			\StrGobbleLeft{##1}{1}[\restofline]%
			\par % End the previous verse
			\iffirstverse
				% \penalty10000    % Lock Verse 1 and Verse 2 together
				% \penalty3000    % Separate Verse 1 and 2
				\penalty500    % Separate Verse 1 and 2
				\clubpenalty=150 % REVERT TO 150 for all subsequent verses
				\firstversefalse
			\fi
			\hspace*{.25em}% Start the new verse
			\ifblue
				{\color{Blue}\textbf{\firstletter}}%
				\bluefalse
			\else
				{\color{Red}\textbf{\firstletter}}%
				\bluetrue
			\fi
			\restofline%
		}
		\renewcommand{\\}{\processline}
		\psalmtext
		\par 
	\endgroup
}

\lettrine[lines=2]{\zallmancaps \color{Blue} W}{hile} Psalm \psalm{148} is being said at Matins: the one who is to read the Calendar approaches the prelate, asking him softly: \emph{Chapter?} If he says \emph{No}: the Calendar shall be read in the choir, when Matins is finished. But if he says \emph{After Prime}: then it is to be reserved until Prime. But if he says \emph{Yes}: it shall be read in the chapter house after Matins.
\newline Therefore, when it is to be read in the chapter house after Matins: when the \emph{Benedictus} is begun, he must ring for chapter, for long enough that the brothers can conveniently notice that it is being rung for chapter. This manner of ringing must always be observed in convoking the chapter. Furthermore, he must prepare the light in the chapter house so carefully that, while chapter is taking place, no one needs to be occupied with it again. He must also provide that rods for disciplines are not lacking. Having prepared the book, the light for himself to read, the tablet for the time, and other things pertaining to his duty: standing before the lectern from which he is to read, while the community enters the chapter house, he should step aside slightly as the prelate passes by out of reverence for him. Then returning to that place, when the community has entered: let him begin to read in a moderate voice what is to be read, according to the manner written below. When everything has been read, the book closed, and other necessary matters briefly dispatched: let him go to his place, and when chapter is finished, return what is to be returned elsewhere.
\newline When, however, it is reserved until after Prime, while the last psalm is being said at Prime: let the one who is to read approach the prelate, asking him as above. And if he says \emph{Yes}: then let it quickly be rung for chapter before the end of the hour, and let him do everything as above, except regarding the lights, if it is daytime enough that light is not necessary. But if he says \emph{No}: it is to be read in the choir. When it is read in the choir, whether after Matins or after Prime: chapter is not rung, but other things are done in the manner said above, as appropriate.

\newline \emph{Qui vivis et regnas per omnia secula seculorum.}

If twenty-six: the homily of the Gospel \emph{Loquente Ihesu.} is to be repeated on the twenty-fifth Sunday, when it is celebrated for the Sunday, and the homily of the Gospel \emph{Cum sublevasset.} is to be read on the twenty-sixth. But if twenty-seven: the homily of the Gospel \emph{Loquente Ihesu.} is to be read on the twenty-fourth and twenty-fifth Sundays, and the homily of the Gospel \emph{Cum sublevasset.} on the twenty-sixth and twenty-seventh. This, however, must be known: that when there are twenty-six or twenty-seven weeks, after the homily has been read in its first week, whether on Sunday or on some weekday, if on the following Sunday on which it ought to have been repeated, a feast occurs, or an octave, it is not to be repeated further.

Excepted is the feast of Blessed Peter Martyr and the feast of the Translation of Blessed Dominic, when either of them occurs on the vigil of Ascension, because then it shall not have second Vespers.
\newline If, however, a totum duplex feast occurs to be celebrated on the day after a simplex or semiduplex or duplex feast: the totum duplex feast shall be begun from the beginning at first Vespers, and of the feast of that day a commemoration only shall be made.
\newline If, however, a semiduplex feast occurs after a semiduplex or simplex feast, on the day of the first feast at Vespers the psalms shall be of that first feast, but the antiphon upon the psalms and the chapter and the things that follow shall be of the following feast, and a commemoration shall be made of the first feast.
\newline When, however, a simplex feast occurs after a semiduplex feast: the semiduplex feast shall have full Vespers on its day, and of the following simplex feast a commemoration only shall be made.
\newline When, however, one simplex feast occurs after another simplex feast: the first feast on its day shall have the antiphon and psalms at Vespers. The chapter, however, and the things that follow shall be of the following feast, and a commemoration shall be made of the first feast.

\newline If a simplex or greater feast is to be celebrated on any Saturday: the feast shall have full Vespers on that Saturday, even if the Sunday does not have second Vespers, except on the Saturdays of Advent, and on the Saturday before Septuagesima and thereafter on Saturdays up to Easter. On which a simplex or semiduplex feast shall have the psalms and antiphon at Vespers, but from the chapter it shall be begun of the Sunday, and a commemoration shall afterwards be made of the feast. A duplex or totum duplex feast, however, shall at all times have full Vespers, and of the following Sunday a commemoration only shall be made.
\newline When, however, a simplex feast occurs after a Sunday: the psalms and antiphons shall be of the Sunday. The chapter and the things that follow of the feast. But if it is semiduplex or duplex: the psalms shall be of the Sunday, but the antiphon upon the psalms and the chapter and the things that follow shall be of the following feast. A totum duplex feast, however, shall be begun from the beginning, and shall have full Vespers, and of the Sunday a commemoration shall be made.
\newline It should be known, moreover, that a Sunday within octaves in all respects is regarded as a simplex feast, except as regards to first Vespers when any simplex feast occurs on a Saturday within octaves.

\selectlanguage{english}
\lettrine[lines=2]{\zallmancaps \color{Blue} I}{t} should be known that for the deceased, vigils of nine lessons are always to be said by the brothers in the community or wherever they may be stationed, once a week: except at the times noted below in which they may be omitted. At Vespers, however, and Matins and Lauds, the antiphons, psalms, canticles, versicles, lessons, and responsories are to be said as noted before in the Commemoration of the Dead: except that the ninth responsory is said only with these three verses, namely, \emph{Dies illa. Tremens.} and \emph{Creator.} After the last verse let the responsory be repeated in its entirety.
\newline The aforesaid vigils of nine lessons are omitted, however, on all Saturdays and on the vigil of any feast, unless it is of three lessons. Likewise on duplex and totum duplex feasts at both Vespers. And on the vigil of the Nativity of the Lord and thereafter up to the octave of Epiphany exclusive. Likewise on Wednesday before Maundy Thursday and thereafter up to Monday after the octave of Easter exclusive. Likewise on the vigil of Ascension and thereafter up to its octave exclusive. Likewise on the vigil of Pentecost and thereafter up to the Sunday after Trinity exclusive. Likewise through all octaves. If, however, there is presently a deceased person: the office of vigils may be performed for him, but not on the day of the Nativity of the Lord, nor on Maundy Thursday, nor on Good Friday, nor on Holy Saturday, nor on Easter Day, nor on the day of Pentecost. But if any anniversary of the Order occurs: whether for a recently deceased person or for any other reason, when the aforesaid vigils are to be said within the week on any day: it suffices in that same week that those are said, and the brothers are not bound to any other [vigil].
\lettrine[lines=2]{\zallmancaps \color{Red} O}{f} vigils of three lessons, however, let the psalms, lessons, and responsories be said in this manner. On Sunday and on Wednesday let there be said the psalms of the first nocturn with their antiphons and versicle and lessons and responsories. On Monday and Thursday let there be said the psalms of the second nocturn with their antiphons, and versicle and lessons and responsories. On Tuesday, however, and Friday let there be said the psalms of the third nocturn, similarly with their antiphons, versicle, lessons, and responsories. In the last responsory, however, let only the first verse be said, namely, \emph{Dies illa.} After the repetition, however, the responsory is not repeated. But if a feast falls in between: the aforesaid order shall nonetheless be kept on individual weekdays.
\newline In this order, moreover, let the Collects be said in vigils of the dead. For a present or for one deceased person: if he was a bishop, let the first Collect be said, \emph{Deus qui inter apostolicos,} if another, \emph{Inclina,} if a woman, \emph{Quesumus domine,} the second, \emph{Deus venie,} the third, \emph{Fidelium.}
For brothers, family members, and benefactors, the first, \emph{Deus venie,} the second, \emph{Deus qui nos patrem,} the third, \emph{Fidelium.}
On every anniversary, the first, \emph{Deus indulgentiarum,} the second, \emph{Deus venie,} the third, \emph{Fidelium.}
If a death and an anniversary occur at the same time, let there be omitted \emph{Deus venie,} and let the first Collect be said for the present deceased person, the second for the anniversary, the third is not changed. In vigils of three lessons, however, let the first be said, \emph{Deus venie,} the second, \emph{Deus qui nos patrem,} the third \emph{Fidelium.}
